# Regulatory, labour-relations and tech-sovereignty constraints for a DK/NO/SE digital order/delivery solution (as of 2026-10-21)

Scope: Company A (Danish construction retailer, DK/NO/SE) rolling out digital order intake (AI extraction), ETA prediction, customer chatbot, driver app with GPS + photo proof-of-delivery (POD), possibly workforce management. Law-firm/vendor sources are flagged "(law firm)" / "(vendor/secondary)". Items marked "[not fetched this session]" are cited to primary legal texts from background knowledge and should be spot-checked before the proposal is submitted.

## 1. GDPR and national rules on driver GPS tracking and photo POD (Datatilsynet DK/NO, IMY SE; Norway aml. kap. 9)

### Takeaway
GPS/fleet tracking and POD photos are lawful in all three countries if they rest on legitimate interest (or a legal obligation), have **purposes defined in advance**, are minimised (no off-duty tracking, real-time fleet view rather than stored history where possible), and are **not later repurposed** to control individual drivers' working time or performance. Each country also layers a labour-law procedure on top (DK 6-week notice; NO drøfting with tillitsvalgte under aml. §9-2; SE MBL negotiation) — see section 3. Employee monitoring is an active enforcement priority in DK and SE in 2025-2026.

### Cited Findings
**Norway (joint guidance from Arbeidstilsynet, Datatilsynet and Havtil with the social partners, "Veileder om kontroll og overvåking i arbeidslivet", 2019)**
- Control measures towards employees are governed by Arbeidsmiljøloven chapter 9 and simultaneously by GDPR (via personopplysningsloven); both regimes must be complied with at once. Measures need a legitimate ("saklig") ground in the business and must not be a disproportionate burden on the employee (aml. §9-1). — [Veileder om kontroll og overvåking 2019 (Havtil copy)](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf); [Arbeidstilsynet copy](https://prep.arbeidstilsynet.no/contentassets/04ec2eb566d44942bd6693e9e3a0c99e/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- The employer must "as early as possible" discuss (drøfte) the need, design, implementation and major changes of control measures with the employees' tillitsvalgte (aml. §9-2(1)); the verneombud must be consulted and the arbeidsmiljøutvalg must handle plans with significant impact. — [Veileder 2019](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- Explicit worked example in the guidance: a company that collects GPS data **to make deliveries to customers more efficient cannot later use the same data to control** its employees (incompatible further purpose). — [Veileder 2019, section on time registration / GPS](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- GPS tracking in work vehicles needs a legal basis: either a legal obligation (driving/rest-time rules, tax rules, employee safety) or a balancing of interests. Multiple purposes are allowed only if **all purposes are described in advance** and the drøfting and information duties are met; the purpose description governs what data may be processed, how long it is stored and who has access. — [Veileder 2019, "Arbeidsgivers overvåking av biler", p. 41](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- For **fleet management (flåtestyring)** whose purpose is real-time dispatch, "it will not be relevant to store the data" — the aim is to know where vehicles are, not where they have been. Detailed sensor data (harsh braking, idling, sharp turns, speed) is seen as intrusive and weighs heavily against the employer; a customer contract or tender requiring such data does **not** widen the employer's right to process it. Employers must know what their tracking units actually record. — [Veileder 2019, p. 41-42](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- Case law: Rt. 2013 s. 143 ("Avfallsservice") – combining GPS data with timesheets to check overtime claims was unlawful processing (though the evidence was admitted and the dismissal upheld); the court noted employers can be fined in such cases. — [Veileder 2019, p. 41](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- Employees must be told that control/monitoring happens, how, what the data is used for, and any change of purpose. — [Veileder 2019](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf)
- Datatilsynet (NO) has reported a sharp rise in disputes about GPS in work vehicles (13 cases in one year, 18+ by mid-year of the next; year not stated in the snippet). — [Arbeidstilsynet/Datatilsynet guidance (search snippet)](https://www.nhosh.no/contentassets/6858b9c0d9b44ee0ad1a7d40569dac49/veileder_om_kontroll_og_overvaking_i_arbeidslivet.pdf)
- Norway applies GDPR via EEA (personopplysningsloven); employer access to e-mail/files and camera surveillance at work are regulated in personopplysningsforskriften (FOR-2018-06-15-876). — [Lovdata, Arbeidsmiljøloven kap. 9](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2005-06-17-62/KAPITTEL_9); [Lovdata, personopplysningsforskriften](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2018-06-15-876) [not fetched this session]

**Denmark**
- Dansk Industri guidance: employers may track vehicles used during working hours where they have a legitimate interest in locating the vehicle, under GDPR art. 6(1)(f) and Databeskyttelsesloven §12(1); tracking must be reasonable and proportionate; data collected to locate vehicles **cannot be used to monitor the person's behaviour or whereabouts** beyond that purpose; where private use is allowed, **it must be possible to switch tracking off**; GPS monitoring must be notified under the DA/LO control-measures agreement. — [DI: GPS-overvågning (employer association)](https://www.danskindustri.dk/vi-radgiver-dig/personale/personalepolitik-persondata-og-kontrolforanstaltninger/gps-overvagning/)
- Datatilsynet (DK) named "overvågning og kontrol af ansatte" as a 2026 focus area for planned inspections. — [shattered.io (secondary, vendor blog)](https://shattered.io/dk/gps-sporing-ansatte-gdpr-boede-2026/) — verify against [datatilsynet.dk](https://www.datatilsynet.dk) before citing.
- The same secondary source reports an Italian €120,000 fine for continuous tracking of five employees' cars incl. off-duty, and €291,800 in total fines from six European DPAs for similar surveillance cases in June 2026. — [shattered.io (secondary)](https://shattered.io/dk/gps-sporing-ansatte-gdpr-boede-2026/) — treat as unverified.

**Sweden**
- IMY position (as summarised by Lawline/FAR): an employer using positioning must show the processing is necessary for the purpose and that no less intrusive alternative exists; accepted purposes include security, resource allocation (dispatch), complaint handling and billing basis; **using GPS to monitor working hours is generally not permitted**, except possibly where serious time-reporting fraud is suspected; employees should be informed in writing and orally **before** the system goes live. — [Lawline: GPS in the car as surveillance (secondary)](https://lawline.se/answers/far-arbetsgivare-anvanda-gpsen-i-bilen-som-overvakning); [FAR Online: GPS-kontroll av arbetstid](https://www.faronline.se/dokument/rattserien/ratt-arbetsratt/g/ra_gpskontrollavarbetstid/)
- IMY made employee monitoring a supervisory focus in 2025. — [Hammarskiöld (law firm)](https://hammarskiold.se/sv/news/overvakning-av-anstallda-i-fokus-for-integritetsskyddsmyndigheten-2025/) (page could not be fetched; title only)

### Inferences
- Design implications for the driver app: (a) define purposes up front (dispatch/ETA, customer notification, POD/complaint handling, legal obligations) and write them into the DPIA and the labour-law notice; (b) tracking only while on shift/on route, with an off switch; (c) ETA model trained on route/vehicle data, not exposed as individual driver performance dashboards; (d) short retention for raw location; (e) POD photos should frame goods/delivery location, avoid capturing driver or bystanders' faces, with defined retention tied to complaint windows. A DPIA (GDPR art. 35) is very likely required given systematic monitoring of employees.
- Using the same GPS data for workforce performance management later would require a new purpose assessment, new drøfting/negotiation and new notice in all three countries — better to decide scope before rollout.

- Could not retrieve Datatilsynet (DK) or IMY primary guidance pages on GPS directly in this session; no direct DPA guidance found specifically on photo proof-of-delivery.
- Year of the Norwegian "13 → 18 cases" statistic not confirmed.

## 2. EU AI Act timeline (incl. Digital Omnibus), risk tiers for planned uses, Norway via EEA

### Takeaway
The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) postponed Annex III high-risk obligations from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027** (Annex I-linked: 2 Aug 2028). Prohibitions (Feb 2025), GPAI (Aug 2025) and **Art. 50 chatbot transparency (2 Aug 2026)** are already applicable. Of Company A's uses, only AI-driven workforce management (task allocation/performance monitoring of drivers) is plausibly high-risk (Annex III employment); the chatbot carries Art. 50 disclosure duties; order extraction and ETA prediction are minimal-risk. Norway has **not** yet incorporated the AI Act into the EEA Agreement; a Norwegian KI-lov is planned for the Storting in spring 2027.

### Cited Findings
- AI Act = Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Prohibited practices and AI-literacy duty apply from 2 Feb 2025; GPAI model obligations from 2 Aug 2025. — [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 113](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) [not fetched this session]
- Digital Omnibus on AI: political agreement 7 May 2026; EP endorsement 16 June 2026; Council adoption 29 June 2026; published in OJ 24 July 2026 as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026. — [Usercentrics (vendor)](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Orrick (law firm)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)
- Annex III (Art. 6(2)) high-risk obligations now apply **2 Dec 2027** instead of 2 Aug 2026; Annex I (Art. 6(1)) systems **2 Aug 2028**; Art. 50(2) synthetic-content marking **2 Dec 2026**; new prohibitions (NCII/"nudifier" and CSAM-generating systems) from 2 Dec 2026; a "small mid-cap" category (≤750 employees, ≤€150m turnover) gets simplified compliance. — [Orrick (law firm)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)
- Art. 50 transparency obligations were **not** amended by the Omnibus; deployer-facing duties apply from Aug 2026. — [Usercentrics (vendor)](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)
- Art. 50(1): providers must design AI systems that interact directly with people so users are informed they are interacting with an AI, unless obvious. Annex III point 4 (employment, workers management) lists AI used to allocate tasks based on individual behaviour or personal traits and to monitor and evaluate performance and behaviour of workers as high-risk. Art. 26(7): deployers that are employers must inform workers' representatives and affected workers before putting a high-risk AI system into service at the workplace. — [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) [not fetched this session]
- Denmark: Act No. 467 of 14 May 2025 on supplementary provisions to the AI Act entered into force 2 Aug 2025 (first EU member state); competent authorities are Digitaliseringsstyrelsen (also single point of contact), Datatilsynet and Domstolsstyrelsen. — [Lex Mundi AI Legislative Guide – Denmark (law-firm network)](https://www.lexmundi.com/guides/ai-legislative-guide/jurisdictions/europe/denmark/); [Lovguiden – lovforslag](https://www.lovguiden.dk/dokument/retsinfo/forslag-til-lov-om-supplerende-bestemmelser-til-forordningen-om-kunstig-intelligens)
- Sweden: supplementary rules proposed in SOU 2025:101; nine sectoral market-surveillance authorities proposed, with PTS as coordinating authority and PTS, IMY and Finansinspektionen also overseeing high-risk systems; not yet formally adopted at time of the source. — [Lex Mundi – Sweden](https://www.lexmundi.com/guides/ai-legislative-guide/jurisdictions/europe/sweden/); [Setterwalls (law firm), 24 Nov 2025](https://setterwalls.se/artikel/a-brief-update-on-swedens-adaptations-to-the-ai-act-sou-2025101?pagenr=8)
- Norway: as of October 2026 the AI Act has not been incorporated into the EEA Agreement and the Norwegian KI-lov has not been passed; first hearing ran 30 June–30 Sept 2025; in Aug 2026 the government announced a new hearing (autumn 2026) due to the EU changes, aiming to put the bill to the Storting in spring 2027. Nkom is designated coordinating supervisory authority/national contact point. Norwegian firms placing AI systems on the EU market, or whose AI output is used in the EU, are already in scope. — [Opsio (vendor/secondary)](https://opsiocloud.com/no/knowledge-base/ki-loven-ai-act-norge/); [CMS Norway (law firm)](https://cms.law/en/nor/publication/teknologinytt-hva-er-kunstig-intelligens-reguleringen-ai-act-og-naar-blir-den-norsk-lov); [TU/NTB](https://www.tu.no/nyhetsstudio/nye-eu-regler-for-ki-norge-og-eoes-henger-ennaa-langt-etter/127027)

### Inferences (risk tiering of Company A's planned uses — consultant judgement, not legal advice)
| Use | Likely tier | Key obligations |
|---|---|---|
| AI order extraction (e-mail/PDF/phone → order lines) | Minimal risk | AI literacy (Art. 4); GDPR on customer data; human check on low-confidence extractions is good practice |
| ETA prediction / route optimisation (vehicle-level) | Minimal risk, **unless** outputs are used to evaluate individual drivers | Keep as vehicle/route model; avoid per-driver scoring |
| Customer chatbot (LLM) | Limited risk – Art. 50 | Disclose AI interaction (applies now, from 2 Aug 2026); label generated content where relevant (50(2) from 2 Dec 2026, provider duty) |
| Workforce management: AI shift/task allocation or driver performance evaluation | **High-risk (Annex III pt. 4)** | Applies from 2 Dec 2027: deployer duties (human oversight, logs, inform worker reps per Art. 26(7), FRIA not required for private deployers in general) — plus labour-law consultation now |
- Practical timing: a 2026-2027 rollout of order/ETA/chatbot is not blocked by high-risk rules; if workforce-AI is in scope, design it to be compliant by Dec 2027 or keep it rule-based/decision-support.
- Norway: legal uncertainty on timing, but designing to the EU standard group-wide avoids rework once the EEA incorporation happens.

## 3. Labour relations: consultation duties when introducing technology/control measures (SE MBL, DK agreements, NO aml./Hovedavtalen)

### Takeaway
All three countries require worker involvement **before** the decision/rollout: Sweden – primary negotiation under MBL §11 before deciding important changes (a new driver app/GPS system qualifies in practice) plus continuous information (§19); Denmark – at least **6 weeks' notice** of new control measures (DA/LO agreement) and samarbejdsudvalg principles for personal data; Norway – drøfting with tillitsvalgte "as early as possible" (aml. §9-2) plus Hovedavtalen rules. Build 2–3 months of consultation per country into the plan.

### Cited Findings
- **Sweden, MBL §11**: employer must on its own initiative negotiate with the union party to the collective agreement **before deciding** on important changes to the business or to working/employment conditions of members. **MBL §19**: continuous, proactive information on business developments (production, finances, personnel policy). — [Naturvetarna – MBL](https://www.naturvetarna.se/fortroendevald/din-verktygslada/lagar/mbl); [ST – MBL (PDF)](https://www.st.org/api/files/2026-02/MBL.pdf); [Labour Court AD 2008 nr 76](https://lagen.nu/dom/ad/2008:76)
- **Denmark**: DA/LO agreement on control measures covers all monitoring including GPS; new control measures must be notified **at least 6 weeks before** implementation (exceptions only where the purpose would be defeated or compelling operational reasons apply, with notice as soon as possible). DA/LO recommend local agreements on how GPS may be used. Under the DA/LO Samarbejdsaftale (pt. 3) the samarbejdsudvalg must set principles for the company's collection, storage and use of personal data. — [DI: Generelt om kontrolforanstaltninger](https://www.danskindustri.dk/vi-radgiver-dig/personale/personalepolitik-persondata-og-kontrolforanstaltninger/generelt-om-kontrolforanstaltninger/); [3F: Aftale om kontrolforanstaltninger (PDF)](https://www.3f.dk/-/media/files/artikler/dit-arbejdsliv/aftaleomkontrolforanstaltninger.pdf); [DI notice template, June 2025](https://www.danskindustri.dk/vi-radgiver-dig/di-dokumenter-for-personale/personalejuras-dokumenter/skabeloner/diverse-skabeloner/varsling-af-kontrolforanstaltninger/)
- **Norway**: aml. §9-2 – drøfting with tillitsvalgte as early as possible on need, design, implementation and major changes; information to all affected before implementation; verneombud consulted; AMU handles plans with significant impact. — [Veileder 2019](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/7e03b969a3dc4eb687a9e8fa997fe48b/veileder-om-kontroll-og-overvaking-2019.pdf); [Lovdata aml. kap. 9](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2005-06-17-62/KAPITTEL_9)
- EU overlay: AI Act Art. 26(7) requires employers deploying high-risk AI to inform worker representatives and affected workers beforehand. — [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) [not fetched this session]

### Inferences
- Rollout sequencing: DK pilot can start ≥6 weeks after notice; SE requires MBL §11 negotiation concluded before the go-live decision (negotiations can take weeks; failure to negotiate risks damages); NO needs drøfting early in design. Recommend involving union/tillitsvalgte in design sprints, which also improves driver adoption.
- Applicability depends on collective-agreement coverage (likely in Nordic construction/logistics retail); in SE MBL §11 applies toward unions the employer has a collective agreement with.

### Gaps
- Exact Norwegian Hovedavtalen (LO–NHO) chapter IX provisions on technology/control measures not retrieved; specific Danish Samarbejdsaftale clause on new technology (beyond pt. 3 data principles) not retrieved.

## 4. Data sovereignty / hosting: EU Data Act, DPF/Schrems II, NIS2, sovereign cloud, Netcompany positioning

### Takeaway
EU-US transfers rest on the Data Privacy Framework (upheld by the General Court Sept 2025, appeal C-703/25 P pending at the CJEU) — a live "Schrems III" risk to plan around with EU hosting and SCC fallback. The EU Data Act has applied since 12 Sept 2025 (cloud switching, IoT/vehicle data access). NIS2 is in force in DK (1 July 2025) and SE (15 Jan 2026); Norway has only NIS1 (digitalsikkerhetsloven, 1 Oct 2025). Building-materials retail is likely out of NIS2 scope, but in-house logistics may need a check. Netcompany explicitly positions on "European sovereignty for the age of AI".

### Cited Findings
- DPF: General Court dismissed Latombe's challenge on 3 Sept 2025; appeal lodged 31 Oct 2025 (Case C-703/25 P), pending; Microsoft intervened in support of the Commission (reported June 2026). — [WilmerHale (law firm), 1 Dec 2025](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/20251201-european-court-of-justice-to-review-challenge-to-eu-us-data-privacy-framework); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/35459-latombe-filed-appeal-against-general-court-dismissal-of-challenge-to-european-unionunited-states-data-protection-framework-adequacy-decision-in-latombe-v-commission); [ieu-monitoring, 28 June 2026](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/microsoft-backs-eu-commission-in-eu-court-case-on-transatlantic-data-flows-and-privacy/1244467/) — **may change**: a CJEU ruling could come in 2026-2027.
- EU Data Act = Regulation (EU) 2023/2854, applicable from 12 Sept 2025 (connected-product data access, cloud-switching/egress rules). — [EUR-Lex 2023/2854](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj) [not fetched this session]
- NIS2 Denmark: Act No. 434 of 6 May 2025 (general NIS2 act) plus sector acts, in force 1 July 2025. — [Bird & Bird (law firm)](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/denmark/update-from-the-nordic-countries-on-the-nis2-directive-implementation); [nis-2-directive.com](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/Transposition/Denmark.html)
- NIS2 Sweden: Cybersäkerhetslagen (SFS 2025:1506), issued 11 Dec 2025, in force 15 Jan 2026. — [nisd2.eu (secondary)](https://nisd2.eu/en/wiki/timelines-and-status/nis2-status-sweden); [nis-solutions.eu](https://nis-solutions.eu/countries/sweden/)
- Norway: implemented NIS1 via digitalsikkerhetsloven on 1 Oct 2025; NIS2 not yet in the EEA Agreement, no Norwegian date. — [secomea (secondary)](https://secomea.com/blog/compliance/nis2-compliance-in-europe-country-by-country/); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/denmark/update-from-the-nordic-countries-on-the-nis2-directive-implementation)

- Netcompany: tagline "Let's build European sovereignty for the age of AI!"; accelerated investment in Feniks AI for "sovereign and secure agentic AI delivery"; EASLEY AI platform deployed on Denmark's Gefion supercomputer as a sovereign AI solution for public authorities keeping data in European data centres. CEO André Rogaczewski frames AI sovereignty as strategic for businesses and governments. — [Netcompany Q1 2026 interim report (via Placera)](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/netcompany-netcompany-interim-report-for-the-three-months-ended-31-march-2026-20260506); [GCN, Dec 2025](https://gcn.com/denmark-deploys-netcompanys-sovereign-ai-system/15723); [CFOtech](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/denmark-launches-sovereign-ai-on-gefion-to-boost-public-sector); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/uk-cannot-outsource-ai-future-says-netcompany-boss/)

### Inferences
- Recommend EU-region hosting for driver/customer personal data and an LLM option deployable in EU (or EU sovereign cloud) with US-provider fallback under SCCs + TIA; this hedges a possible CJEU invalidation of DPF and aligns with Netcompany's own sovereignty pitch.
- Data Act: telematics data from connected delivery vehicles/devices may be subject to user data-access rights; cloud contracts must allow switching — favours portable architecture.
- NIS2: Company A probably not an "essential/important entity" as a building-materials retailer; if it operates its own courier/delivery business for third parties, assess "postal and courier services". Either way, suppliers to NIS2 entities face supply-chain security clauses.

## 5. Other: e-invoicing/Peppol, European Accessibility Act, languages

### Takeaway
Norway mandates B2B e-invoicing (EHF/Peppol BIS 3.0) from **1 Jan 2027** — directly relevant to order-to-cash. Denmark requires certified digital bookkeeping systems able to send/receive e-invoices (from 1 Jan 2026; custom ERP by 1 July 2026) but no general B2B e-invoicing mandate. Sweden has no domestic B2B mandate (inquiry due 30 Nov 2027); ViDA cross-border rules from 1 July 2030. The EAA has applied to consumer e-commerce since 28 June 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Norway: all B2B invoices must be electronic (EHF 3.0 / Peppol BIS) from 1 Jan 2027 for VAT-registered businesses (B2C excluded); Ministry of Finance confirmed the accelerated timeline 16 March 2026; by 1 Jan 2030 businesses must keep records electronically and be able to receive/process e-invoices automatically. — [VATupdate, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/03/23/nordic-e-invoicing-and-e-reporting-updates-q1-2026/); [ublbuddy (secondary)](https://ublbuddy.app/en-US/blog/e-invoicing-norway)
- Denmark: no domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate yet; certified digital bookkeeping systems capable of issuing/receiving e-invoices mandatory from 1 Jan 2026 (custom/in-house ERP until 1 July 2026); format moving from OIOUBL 2.1 to NemHandel BIS (Peppol-based) by mid-2029; OIOUBL 3.0 cancelled (Erhvervsstyrelsen, 14 Jan 2026). — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/03/23/nordic-e-invoicing-and-e-reporting-updates-q1-2026/); [marosavat](https://marosavat.com/vat-news/e-invoicing-denmark-complete-guide) — note: marosavat claims B2B e-invoicing became "mandatory for all businesses by 1 Jan 2026", **contradicted** by VATupdate (mandate is for digital bookkeeping, not e-invoice exchange). Prefer VATupdate; verify at [erhvervsstyrelsen.dk](https://erhvervsstyrelsen.dk).
- Sweden: B2G e-invoicing mandatory since 2019 (Peppol BIS); no B2B mandate; government inquiry launched 5 Feb 2026, due 30 Nov 2027; EU ViDA cross-border rules from 1 July 2030. — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/03/23/nordic-e-invoicing-and-e-reporting-updates-q1-2026/); [igdrasil (secondary)](https://igdrasil.se/en/resources/guides/peppol-einvoice-archiving-sweden/)
- European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) applies to services provided after 28 June 2025; e-commerce services to consumers are expressly in scope; microenterprises (<10 staff and ≤€2m turnover or balance sheet) providing services are exempt. — [Greenberg Traurig (law firm), July 2025](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/7/european-accessibility-act-compliance-what-businesses-in-the-eu-market-need-to-know); [EUR-Lex 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj) [not fetched this session]

### Inferences
- Order-to-cash design must output EHF/Peppol BIS 3.0 invoices in Norway by Jan 2027 and Peppol-compatible formats in DK; a single Peppol access point across the three countries is the natural architecture.
- EAA: B2C-facing web shop, app and chatbot should meet EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA; B2B-only professional (trade) portals are arguably outside the "consumer" e-commerce scope, but designing to the same standard is low-cost. Norway's EEA incorporation/national implementation of the EAA was not confirmed (Norway already has its own universal-design rules for ICT).
- Language: customer-facing chatbot, app and notices (GDPR art. 12-13 transparency, labour-law notices) need Danish, Norwegian (bokmål) and Swedish; Danish/Norwegian/Swedish LLM quality should be tested per language.
