# Economics and pain points of materials delivery to construction sites (contractors) and cost of delivery failures (builders merchants / B2B distributors)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Source-type tags: [STUDY] = academic / independent research / statistics office; [ANALYST] = consultancy or analyst firm (McKinsey, Gartner, FMI); [ASSOC] = trade association or association-commissioned survey; [VENDOR] = vendor marketing or vendor-commissioned (treat as indicative only); [MEDIA] = trade press reporting on a study.

## Q1. How much time and money do contractors lose to late, incomplete or wrong materials deliveries?

### Takeaway
Independent Nordic and UK studies agree that a large share of site labour time is non-productive, and that waiting for or handling materials is one of the biggest causes. Roughly 10–26% of site time goes to waiting, and about 25% to material handling. In one Swedish study only 38% of deliveries were fully correct. Most of the solid Nordic evidence is from before 2020 and should be flagged as older. Recent (2021–2024) global surveys show that material delays are the leading cause of project delays.

### Cited Findings
**Nordic (most relevant to Company A)**
- [STUDY, 2016] Linköping University (Micael Thunberg, PhD research on construction logistics). On one studied project, only **38% of materials arrived at the right time, in the right quantity, at the right place, in the right condition and with correct documentation**. Material problems are described as taking up a large part of construction workers' day. — [Linköping University: Samordning får fart på bygget](https://liu.se/artikel/samordning-far-fart-pa-bygget)
- [STUDY, Swedish, via search summary; underlying document not verified by me] More than one third of all incoming deliveries are defective in some way. About **25% of a craftsman's workday goes to material handling**. Searching for and moving materials on site costs roughly **SEK 50,000 per month**. Chalmers research found only **17.5% of time is direct value-creating work and 25.8% is waiting**. — summary of SBUF/Swedish sources, e.g. [SBUF 17-35 Planering för effektivt byggande](https://vpp.sbuf.se/Public/Documents/InfoSheets/PublishedInfoSheet/f08116c0-3868-43e5-815c-2b654211021f/%2017-35_Planering%20f%C3%B6r%20effektivt%20byggande.pdf). NOTE: I could not open the full text to tie each number to its exact page. The report writer should cite these with caution or treat them as "Swedish industry studies report…".
- [STUDY, 2009 – OLDER] SBUF Informerar 09:14 (PEAB + Halmstad University, 7 housing sites). **Materials are about 50% of the costs of a building project**. Builders-merchant deliveries to site make up **close to 50% of the contract cost** in a normal housing project, yet the supplier is "rarely seen as an actor in the building process" and is usually judged on low price alone. Earlier studies suggest material and material-handling costs **could be cut by nearly 50%**. Case examples: items missing from a delivery forced re-planning, and a **mobile crane sometimes had to be booked twice**. Back-orders (restnoteringar) disrupted subcontractors. Suppliers complained that contractors do not tell them in time when the schedule slips. — [SBUF Informerar 09:14 (PDF)](https://vpp.sbuf.se/Public/Documents/InfoSheets/PublishedInfoSheet/a5c01e4f-f428-4577-aaae-d94ece005040/SBUF%200914.pdf)
- [STUDY, ~2017 – OLDER] Aarhus University "Revalue" project: more than 1,000 hours of observing craftsmen on 4 Danish sites. **About 66% of the workday was waste** (non-value-adding). Productivity ranged from 50% (plumbers) to 18% (floor layers). The main causes were poor planning and coordination between trades. The professor said 50–60% productivity is realistic with simple lean tools. — [Ingeniøren / 3F note, 2018](https://ing.dk/note/ny-undersoegelse-23-af-haandvaerkernes-arbejdsdag-er-spildtid). NOTE: the article URL/headline says "23 % af håndværkernes arbejdsdag er spildtid", but the fetched text reported 66% waste. The two may measure different things (for example, 23% pure idle time vs 66% all non-value-adding time). Check this before quoting.
- [MEDIA, 2021] Danish construction waste on course for record levels, possibly costing **more than DKK 66 billion a year** (headline). Up to **30% of materials delivered to Danish sites end up as waste** (search summary). — [Ingeniøren 2021](https://ing.dk/artikel/spild-i-byggeriet-paa-vej-mod-nye-rekorder-kan-koste-over-66-mia-kr-aarligt); [DI Byggeri, Jan 2026](https://www.danskindustri.dk/brancher/di-byggeri/nyheder/arkiv/nyheder/2026/1/mindre-spild-og-mere-ansvar--byggebranchen-arbejder-intensivt-pa-at-nedbringe-affaldsmangderne/)
- [ASSOC, 2006–2007 – OLDER, supply-crisis context] Danish industry survey: in 2006, **80% of construction companies had to decline orders or postpone tasks because of missing or late materials**. This fell to 30% by 2007. — [Ingeniøren 2007](https://ing.dk/artikel/nemmere-skaffe-byggematerialer)

**UK / global**
- [ASSOC, OLDER, likely pre-2010] Construction Products Association report (via Building magazine): **site workers waste about 10% of their time waiting for or collecting materials**. Poor logistics planning costs UK construction **up to GBP 3bn a year**. A consolidation-centre case cut **waiting time from 17% to 3%**. Waiting by trade: bricklayers about 45 min/day, carpenters 62, roofers 75, electricians 80, plumbers 83 (the source for the by-trade figures may be a different study). — [Building.co.uk "Wait not, want not"](https://www.building.co.uk/wait-not-want-not/3037593.article) (paywalled; numbers taken from search snippet)
- [STUDY] Work-sampling study of a civil works project: waiting for materials, equipment, tools and instructions was **46% of non-contributory time**, the largest single loss category. — [UPB repository](https://repository.upb.edu.co/handle/20.500.11912/6326)
- [ANALYST + VENDOR, 2021] Autodesk/FMI "Harnessing the Data Advantage in Construction" (3,900+ respondents, data for 2020). "Bad data" cost global construction **USD 1.85 trillion in 2020**. Decisions based on bad data caused **USD 88.69bn of rework (14% of all rework)**. Autodesk is a software vendor and co-sponsor. — [Autodesk/FMI press release](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/autodesk-fmi-study-global-construction-industry-data-strategies/)
- [ANALYST + VENDOR, 2018 – OLDER] FMI/PlanGrid "Construction Disconnected": about USD 177bn a year in US labour costs lost to non-productive activities, and roughly 35% of time spent on such activities (looking for project information, conflict resolution, rework). These numbers are from memory of the widely cited report. I did not re-fetch the page, so check them on the page before using. — [Autodesk blog: Construction Disconnected](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/construction-disconnected-fmi-report/)
- [ASSOC + VENDOR] AGC & Autodesk survey: **about 88% of US construction firms had project delays, and 75% cited longer lead times or shortages of materials**. — via [ForConstructionPros](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/news/21771916/report-construction-optimism-high-despite-severe-challenges)
- [VENDOR survey] Sherwin-Williams: **about 87% of respondents on projects over USD 30m had delays; 71% named materials** as a main driver (weather 65%). — [ForConstructionPros](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/latest-news/article/22938861/sherwinwilliams-company-9-in-10-construction-pros-face-costly-delays-sherwinwilliams-survey)
- [ASSOC/MEDIA, 2022] Civil Quarterly survey: civil contractors hit by material problems rose from 43% (Q3 2020) to 94% (Q1 2022). **71% reported moderate to severe delays in getting materials/equipment**. — via [ForConstructionPros](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/news/21771916/report-construction-optimism-high-despite-severe-challenges)
- [VENDOR] IronmongeryDirect (UK merchant): failed deliveries cost UK tradespeople **about GBP 13.6m a year in lost time**, because jobs are cancelled or delayed. It cites average UK on-time delivery of 92%. — [Builders Merchants News](https://www.buildersmerchantsnews.co.uk/news/news-archive/ironmongerydirect-saves-customers-efficient-service)

### Inferences
- Labour is the expensive input in the Nordics, and builders-merchant deliveries are about half of contract cost (SBUF). Even a small share of crew time lost to a late or incomplete delivery is therefore worth much more to a contractor than the price difference between merchants. This supports competing on reliability rather than price.
- The "crane booked twice" example is the typical knock-on cost. A delivery failure also strands equipment, subcontractors and the schedule, not only the materials.
- Several sources point to a lack of information flow (the merchant does not know about schedule changes, and the contractor does not know where the order is). This is a visibility problem as well as a logistics one.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2020+) Nordic study that measures hours lost per late delivery. The Nordic evidence comes from 2009–2017 academic and SBUF work.
- I found no McKinsey or Dodge figure tied specifically to materials-delivery waiting time. McKinsey's "Reinventing construction" (2017) is about general productivity and was not fetched.
- I found no SINTEF, BUILD (Aalborg) or Byggföretagen figure in this round.

## Q2. What do contractors and trade customers value most when choosing a builders merchant?

### Takeaway
Availability and delivery reliability rank at or near the top next to price. B2B buyers increasingly expect digital self-service and omnichannel, and say they will switch suppliers to get it.

### Cited Findings
- [ASSOC/consultancy, April 2026; small sample] Growtivation survey of 45 builders'-merchant employees at 19 companies. **Reliable stock availability was the most important factor in a strong supplier relationship (91% put it in their top 3)**. Reasons for lost sales: **cheaper alternative 84%, product out of stock 60%, delivery or lead time too long 38%**. — [Builders Merchants News](https://www.buildersmerchantsnews.co.uk/news/suppliers/pressure-prices-means-stocking-vital)
- [ASSOC, 2019 – OLDER] ECA (UK Electrical Contractors' Association) contractor survey of wholesalers. Ranking: **1) quality of working relationship, 2) timeliness of site delivery, 3) price competitiveness**. — [EDA: ECA survey 2019](https://www.eda.org.uk/news/eca-survey-2019/)
- [ANALYST, 2016 – OLDER] L.E.K. European Building Contractor Survey: contractor purchase drivers at builders' merchants, including availability, delivery and breadth of range. L.E.K. says merchants can use availability, delivery speed and range to justify higher prices. — [L.E.K. European Building Contractor Survey 2016](https://www.lek.com/insights/ei/european-building-contractor-survey-2016-value-creation-opportunities-building)
- [ANALYST, 2024] McKinsey B2B Pulse (more than 300,000 data points). B2B customers are **"more willing than ever to switch suppliers"** to get a great omnichannel experience. All segments prefer omnichannel. The bar is now **10+ channels across in-person, remote and self-service, 24/7**. Performance guarantees and real-time customer service are among the 5 capabilities customers most want. — [McKinsey: The new B2B growth equation](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-new-b2b-growth-equation)
- [ANALYST, 2025] Gartner survey of 632 B2B buyers (Aug–Sep 2024): **61% prefer an overall rep-free buying experience**. Self-service is preferred for information tasks. — [Gartner press release, 25 June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)

### Inferences
- Price wins individual quotes, but availability and delivery reliability decide which merchant becomes the contractor's preferred merchant. Rep-free and self-service preferences mean contractors want to check order and delivery status themselves rather than phone the branch.

### Gaps
- I found no public STARK, Beijer, Byggmax, Travis Perkins or Ferguson customer survey with numbers in this round. Their annual reports (customer NPS, digital share) are worth checking separately.
- I found no Scandinavian contractor survey that ranks merchant-selection criteria.

## Q3. WISMO ("where is my order") share of customer-service contacts, and cost per contact

### Takeaway
In e-commerce and retail, WISMO is the largest single contact category, at about 40–60% of inbound contacts and up to 80% for some retailers. The benchmarks are B2C and from vendors. I found no B2B-distribution-specific figure.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] WISMO is **40–60% of all inbound e-commerce support contacts**. Each ticket costs **USD 5–15**: email 5–10, chat 4–10, phone 7–16 (outsourced BPO). Claims a 60–80% volume reduction is possible. — [Ringly: WISMO tickets](https://www.ringly.io/blog/wismo-tickets)
- [VENDOR] For some retailers, **up to 80% of contacts are WISMO**. An average WISMO contact costs **GBP 4.50–8.00**. 500 WISMO contacts a week equals **GBP 117k–208k a year**. — [Sorted: The real cost of the retail WISMO problem](https://sorted.com/?p=13545)
- [VENDOR] Salesforce explainer on WISMO (definitions, customer-service burden). — [Salesforce: What is WISMO?](https://www.salesforce.com/uk/commerce/wismo/)

### Inferences
- A B2B builders merchant gets phone calls from both site foremen and office buyers, so the cost per contact is probably at the high (phone) end and also takes branch or sales staff away from selling. This is an inference, not measured.

### Gaps
- No B2B distribution or builders-merchant WISMO share was found. Company A should measure it from its own contact-reason data. This is a strong discovery-phase recommendation.

## Q4. Cost of a failed or re-delivered delivery, and failed-delivery rates

### Takeaway
Parcel-level studies put first-attempt failure at about 6–8% and cost at about EUR 12–17 per failure. Heavy or bulky builders-merchant deliveries (truck plus crane, booked slots) cost far more per failure. I found no published figure for that segment.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR study, 2021] Loqate (GBG) "Fixing Failed Deliveries": first-time failure rates **US 8% (USD 17.20 each), Germany 7% (EUR 14.69), UK 6% (GBP 11.60)**. 24% of businesses say more than 1 in 10 orders fail at first attempt. 5% of online orders never arrive. 41% blame poor address data. — [Loqate PDF](https://info.loqate.com/hubfs/Loqate%202021/Fixing%20Failed%20Deliveries/Fixing%20Failed%20Deliveries%20-%20Final.pdf); [SDCExec summary](https://www.sdcexec.com/sourcing-procurement/news/21307774/loqate-loqate-study-zeroes-in-on-cost-of-failed-deliveries-despite-ecommerce-growth)
- [STUDY, 2009 – OLDER] Construction example: missing items meant a **mobile crane had to be booked twice** (a second truck and crane visit). — [SBUF 09:14](https://vpp.sbuf.se/Public/Documents/InfoSheets/PublishedInfoSheet/a5c01e4f-f428-4577-aaae-d94ece005040/SBUF%200914.pdf)

### Inferences
- Parcel failure costs (about EUR 15) understate builders-merchant reality. A re-delivery of heavy goods needs another truck (often with a crane) and driver time, plus the contractor's idle crew. A bottom-up estimate in the proposal, using Company A's own truck-hour cost, is better than quoting parcel benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No public failed-delivery rate or cost was found for heavy/bulky or builders-merchant deliveries.

## Q5. Impact of delivery visibility (tracking, ETA, proof of delivery) on satisfaction, contacts and loyalty

### Takeaway
The evidence is almost all vendor case studies (B2C or 3PL). They consistently report 50–80% fewer WISMO contacts after proactive tracking and notifications. They are useful as directional evidence but are not independent.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR case] Wineshipping (3PL) achieved **80% fewer WISMO calls** with AfterShip Tracking. — [CaseStudies.com / AfterShip](https://www.casestudies.com/company/aftership/case-study/wineshippings-journey-and-success-with-aftership-tracking)
- [VENDOR] Sendcloud: a UK retailer cut WISMO queries by **50%** with better tracking access. Proactive notifications cut support tickets by **up to 75%**. — [Sendcloud: reduce WISMO](https://sendcloud.com/reduce-wismo)
- [VENDOR] Delivery-notification and ETA vendors (Locus, CIGO, DispatchTrack, Descartes) report that proactive milestone notifications plus accurate ETAs reduce WISMO. — [Locus](https://locus.sh/blogs/real-time-tracking-wismo-reduction/); [CIGO Tracker](https://cigotracker.com/blog/how-delivery-notifications-reduce-wismo-calls/)
- [ANALYST, 2024] McKinsey: B2B customers will switch suppliers for a better omnichannel experience. Real-time service is among the capabilities they want most. — [McKinsey B2B Pulse](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-new-b2b-growth-equation)

### Inferences
- For contractors, the value of visibility is not only fewer calls. An accurate ETA lets the foreman plan crew and crane around the delivery, which tackles the waiting-time losses in Q1.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) study with numbers was found linking tracking to B2B churn or loyalty in building materials.
- Narvar, Convey and project44 reports were not retrieved in this round.

## Q6. On-time-in-full (OTIF) benchmarks in building materials distribution

### Takeaway
I found no published industry-wide OTIF benchmark for building-materials distribution. The available points are an academic case study (about 90%) and a vendor or company claim (about 95%), against a general "good" range of 95–99%.

### Cited Findings
- [STUDY] Case study of a construction-materials distributor (Peru): **94.10% on time, 95.72% in full, OTIF 90.08%** (baseline before improvement). — [UPC: Standardized work, SLP, 5S and IoT to increase OTIF](https://cris.upc.edu.pe/en/publications/application-of-standardized-work-slp-5s-and-iot-to-increase-otif-/)
- [COMPANY claim] Hancock Lumber (US lumberyards): **95.3% OTIF across 11 lumberyards** (page returned 403, so this is from the search snippet). — [Hancock Lumber: Built to Deliver](https://hancocklumber.com/?p=18653)
- [VENDOR glossary] A "good" OTIF is usually 95–99%, and some sectors target 80–90%. — [nShift: OTIF](https://nshift.com/blog/otif-on-time-in-full)
- [VENDOR/MEDIA] UK average on-time delivery is about 92%. — [Builders Merchants News / IronmongeryDirect](https://www.buildersmerchantsnews.co.uk/news/news-archive/ironmongerydirect-saves-customers-efficient-service)
- [STUDY, 2016] Swedish project example: only **38% of deliveries were "perfect"** (time, quantity, place, condition, documents), which is a stricter definition than OTIF. — [Linköping University](https://liu.se/artikel/samordning-far-fart-pa-bygget)

### Inferences
- An OTIF of 90% means about 1 in 10 site deliveries is late or incomplete. With Nordic crew costs (Q7), this justifies an OTIF and visibility programme. It is the gap between merchant-measured OTIF (about 90–95%) and the site-experienced "perfect delivery" rate (38%) that matters.

### Gaps
- No STARK, Beijer, Byggmax or Saint-Gobain Distribution Nordic OTIF figures were found.

## Q7. Nordic-specific factors (labour cost, geography, seasonality)

### Takeaway
Labour costs in Denmark and Norway are among the highest in Europe (Denmark EUR 50.1/h for the whole economy in 2024, and Norway is also above EUR 50). Every hour a crew waits for materials is therefore very expensive. I did not get country-level figures for the construction sector.

### Cited Findings
- [STUDY – Eurostat, 2024] Hourly labour costs (whole economy): **EU EUR 33.5, euro area EUR 37.3, Luxembourg EUR 55.2, Denmark EUR 50.1 (2nd highest in the EU)**. **EU construction sector: EUR 30.0** (euro area 33.4). — [Eurostat news 28 Mar 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250328-1)
- [MEDIA, citing Eurostat 2024] **Norway, Iceland and Denmark are among the top 4 in Europe, each above EUR 50/h**. — [Euronews, Apr 2025](https://euronews.com/business/2025/04/19/labour-costs-across-europe-where-are-they-highest-and-lowest)
- [STUDY – Eurostat] Sweden has the 2nd-highest share of non-wage costs in the EU (31.6%). Sweden's exact hourly figure was not retrieved. — [Eurostat news 28 Mar 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250328-1)
- [STUDY – Eurostat, Q3 2025] Construction labour-cost growth year on year: Denmark +2.6%, Sweden +3.4%, EU construction +4.7%. Norway total +4.7%. — [Eurostat euro indicators, Dec 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/3-17122025-bp)

### Inferences (illustrative calculation, to be labelled as an assumption in the proposal)
- Assume a Danish crew of 4 waits 1.5 hours for a late delivery, costed at about EUR 50/h (whole-economy proxy). That is about EUR 300 per incident before knock-on costs such as a second crane booking, subcontractor idle time or schedule slip. As a further assumption, a contractor taking 10 deliveries a week at 90% OTIF has about 1 failure a week, or about EUR 15k a year. This calculation is mine, not from a source.
- Construction-specific hourly cost in Denmark is probably below the whole-economy EUR 50 (the EU construction average is below the EU total). The proposal should use Eurostat's lc_lci_lev dataset (NACE F) or Danmarks Statistik / SSB / SCB for exact NACE F figures.

### Gaps
- Construction-sector (NACE F) hourly labour cost for DK, NO and SE individually was not retrieved. Get it from the [Eurostat lc_lci_lev dataset](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/lc_lci_lev/default/table), SSB table 11421 or Danmarks Statistik.
- No sourced data was found on Nordic construction seasonality (summer peak) or delivery distances in this round.
