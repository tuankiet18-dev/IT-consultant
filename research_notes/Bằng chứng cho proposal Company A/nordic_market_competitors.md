# Nordic (DK / NO / SE) building materials, builders merchant and DIY market: evidence base for "Company A"

Research date: 2026-10-21. Currency and year are stated for every figure. Labels used:
- **[HARD]**: read in a primary document (annual report, statistics office, official release) that was opened in full.
- **[PRESS]**: reputable press or trade press, article opened in full.
- **[SNIPPET]**: only seen in a search-engine summary because the page could not be opened (kesko.fi refused connections, paywalls). Check it before using it on a slide.
- **[OLD]**: data older than 2023.

## 1. Market size of building materials / builders merchants / DIY retail per country

### Takeaway
The three markets are each roughly EUR 4-6 bn in builders-merchant/DIY retail sales value. Denmark is about EUR 5-6 bn (2022) and Norway was about NOK 49 bn in 2019 through builders' merchants. In Sweden, 12 major chains turn over roughly SEK 44 bn. Professional (B2B) customers are the larger segment, at about 60% of value. Current (2025) totals sit behind paywalls (Virke Byggevareanalysen 2025, Euroconstruct), so the most recent precise totals available here are partly older.

### Cited Findings
**Denmark**
- Danish building and home-improvement (builders' merchant) market about **EUR 5.7 bn in 2022**. The three largest players (STARK, Bygma, Davidsen) hold about **50%** of the market. [SNIPPET; Kesko market data 2024] — [Kesko news, Aug 2024](https://www.kesko.fi/en/media/news-and-releases/news/2024/kesko-to-significantly-expand-operations-in-denmark-by-acquiring-three-central-building-and-home-improvement-trade-operators)
- Kesko's June 2026 Davidsen investor presentation reportedly sizes the Danish **professional-builders segment at EUR 3.1 bn** and the **consumer segment at EUR 1.8 bn** (about EUR 4.9 bn total, about 63% professional). Davidsen's "12%" share is apparently in the pro segment. [SNIPPET, 2025/26 data; PDF could not be opened] — [Kesko Davidsen visit, June 2026](https://www.kesko.fi/49caa5/globalassets/03-sijoittaja/raporttikeskus/2026/q1/davidsen_visit_june2026online.pdf)
- Note: EUR 5.7 bn (2022) and EUR 4.9 bn (2025/26) probably use different market definitions. Use them as a range, about **DKK 36-43 bn**.

**Norway**
- Builders' merchant (byggevarehus) total market **about NOK 48.9 bn in 2019** (Virke Byggevarehandel annual statistics). **Professional segment = 61% of turnover.** Growth was about 1% from 2018 to 2019. [HARD, OLD 2019] — [Konkurransetilsynet decision 2021/0118 (Løvenskiold/Ivar O. Haugen), Annex 2](https://konkurransetilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/2021_0118-1-OFF-Lovenskiold-Handel-AS-Ivar-O.-Haugen-AS.pdf)
- Market shares in 2019: **Optimera 24.5%**, **Mestergruppen 20.1%** (Byggeriet, Byggtorget, XL-Bygg), **Coop 12%**, **Byggmakker 9.8%**, **Løvenskiold Handel (Maxbo) about 9%**. [HARD, OLD 2019] — [same source](https://konkurransetilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/2021_0118-1-OFF-Lovenskiold-Handel-AS-Ivar-O.-Haugen-AS.pdf)
- Virke reported that byggevarehus turnover fell **11.3%** in the last third of 2023 versus 2022, and called 2023 a year of a sharp fall in the building-materials market. [PRESS, Jan 2024] — [TU.no](https://www.tu.no/artikler/virke-frykter-for-byggevarehusene-gar-et-krevende-ar-i-mote/542937)
- Virke's "Byggevareanalysen 2025" covers 2025 turnover, market shares and the pro/private split. It is paywalled (NOK 5,000), so no 2025 figures could be extracted. — [Virke](https://www.virke.no/analyse/statistikk-rapporter/byggevareanalysen/)

**Sweden**
- Byggmaterialindex (Byggmaterialhandlarna / Prognoscentret) is based on **12 major chains with combined annual sales of about SEK 44 bn**. [SNIPPET] — [Placera, Jan 2025](https://www.placera.se/telegram/byggmaterialhandlarna-sag-okad-forsaljning-under-fjarde-kvartalet-2025-01-30)
- **Full year 2025: sales +5.0% in current prices, +1.1% in volume.** This was the first volume increase after four years of decline. Q4 2025: +7.4% current, +4.5% volume, helped by a temporarily increased ROT deduction. [PRESS, 26 Jan 2026] — [Fastighetstidningen](https://fastighetstidningen.se/nyhet/byggmaterialhandeln-bryter-fyra-ars-nedgang)
- Prognoscentret analyst Patric Lindqvist said that "it is many billions to recover" to approach the volumes lost in the decline. — [Fastighetstidningen](https://fastighetstidningen.se/nyhet/byggmaterialhandeln-bryter-fyra-ars-nedgang)
- **Full year 2024: -5.0% current prices, -8.6% volume.** In Q4 2024, consumer sales were +8.8% (current) / +3.9% (fixed) while professional sales "weighed down" the total. [PRESS, 30 Jan 2025] — [Placera](https://www.placera.se/telegram/byggmaterialhandlarna-sag-okad-forsaljning-under-fjarde-kvartalet-2025-01-30)

**Nordic total addressable market (company view)**
- Kesko's Building and Technical Trade division puts the total market for its existing operations in its current market areas (FI, SE, NO, DK, PL, Baltics, including technical trade) at about **EUR 31.6 bn in 2025**. [SNIPPET] — [Kesko B&TT strategy](https://www.kesko.fi/en/investor/strategy/division-strategies/building-and-technical-trade-strategy/)
- STARK Group says RMI makes up **more than 60% of its addressable market** and shows greater cyclical resilience than new build. [HARD] — [STARK Group Annual Report 2025 (FY Aug 2024-Jul 2025)](https://www.stark-deutschland.de/fileadmin/Homepage/nachhaltigkeit/dokumente/nachhaltigkeitsberichte/STARK_Group_Annual_Report_2025.pdf)
- Byggmax describes the Nordic market as fragmented: large chains in metropolitan areas plus many local operators competing on breadth, service and availability. [HARD] — [Byggmax Annual & Sustainability Report 2025](https://mb.cision.com/Main/109/4328714/4017358.pdf)

### Inferences
- A rough triangulation gives a combined DK+NO+SE builders-merchant/DIY market of about **EUR 13-16 bn** (DK about EUR 5 bn, NO about NOK 50-55 bn ≈ EUR 4.5 bn, SE about SEK 44 bn for 12 chains, more for the whole market ≈ EUR 4-5 bn). This is an estimate built from mixed years and definitions. Present it as an order of magnitude only.
- A Company A with "hundreds of stores across DK, NO, SE" would plausibly have a 10-25% share in at least one country, comparable to STARK, Optimera or Davidsen.

### Gaps
- No open-access 2025 total for Norway (Virke paywall) or for Sweden's whole market. Euroconstruct country tables are paywalled.
- Denmark has no official national builders-merchant total. Danmarks Statistik retail series do not isolate building materials in a way that was accessible here.

## 2. Key players: store counts, revenue, B2B share; which real company resembles "Company A"

### Takeaway
STARK Group's Nordic segment (STARK DK, Beijer SE, Neumann/STARK NO, STARK FI) is the clearest real-world analogue: Danish-headquartered, about 265 Nordic branches, EUR 2.9 bn sales, B2B-led with a DIY presence. Other relevant comparisons:
- XL-BYGG: voluntary chain of about 290 stores across DK/NO/SE, the closest match to the footprint pattern.
- Jem & Fix: Danish, 242 stores across DK/SE/NO, but B2C low-price.
- Bygma: Danish, B2B-heavy, but mostly Denmark only.

## 3. Share of revenue from professional/B2B customers in Nordic builders merchants

### Takeaway
Professional customers make up about **60-65% of builders-merchant market value** (Norway 61% in 2019; Danish pro segment about 63% of the market per Kesko 2026). Merchant-type chains such as STARK and Bygma are far above that average. Low-price DIY chains (Byggmax, Jem & Fix) are mostly B2C.

### Cited Findings
- Norway (Virke statistics 2019): **professional segment 61% of byggevarehus turnover.** All chains except Coop sell to both consumers and professionals. [HARD, OLD] — [Konkurransetilsynet 2021](https://konkurransetilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/2021_0118-1-OFF-Lovenskiold-Handel-AS-Ivar-O.-Haugen-AS.pdf)
- Denmark: professional-builders segment **EUR 3.1 bn** versus consumers **EUR 1.8 bn** (about **63% / 37%**). [SNIPPET, Kesko June 2026] — [Kesko Davidsen presentation](https://www.kesko.fi/49caa5/globalassets/03-sijoittaja/raporttikeskus/2026/q1/davidsen_visit_june2026online.pdf)
- STARK Group describes itself as serving "tradespeople" and SME/RMI professionals, and offers credit "to bridge liquidity gaps" plus direct delivery to sites. [HARD] — [STARK AR 2025](https://www.stark-deutschland.de/fileadmin/Homepage/nachhaltigkeit/dokumente/nachhaltigkeitsberichte/STARK_Group_Annual_Report_2025.pdf)
- Byggmax: mainly private consumers, with "a smaller proportion" to professionals. [HARD] — [Byggmax AR 2025](https://mb.cision.com/Main/109/4328714/4017358.pdf)
- In Sweden in 2024/25, consumer sales recovered first while professional sales lagged ("weighed down" the index). [PRESS] — [Placera](https://www.placera.se/telegram/byggmaterialhandlarna-sag-okad-forsaljning-under-fjarde-kvartalet-2025-01-30)
- Kesko: in 2025 sales picked up "especially in the B2B segment" of building and home improvement trade as the construction cycle turned. [SNIPPET] — [Kesko FY2025 presentation](https://www.kesko.fi/4900b8/globalassets/03-sijoittaja/raporttikeskus/2025/q4/kesko-fy2025_presentation_eng.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a mixed merchant like Company A, assuming **about 60-70% B2B / 30-40% B2C** is consistent with market-level data. A STARK-like profile would be higher still, plausibly 75%+ B2B. That last figure is an estimate; no company disclosure was found.
- B2B and B2C cycles are out of phase. B2C/DIY recovered first in 2025 (Sweden). B2B follows construction starts, with a recovery expected in 2026-27.

### Gaps
- No disclosed % B2B for STARK, Bygma, Optimera or Byggmakker in open sources.

## 4. Construction market outlook 2025-2026 per country (new build vs RMI, housing starts)

### Takeaway
All three markets bottomed in 2023-25 and are expected to grow in 2026, though residential new build stays weak:
- **Denmark:** growth is driven by public investment. Residential investment is still down in 2026, but renovation is growing.
- **Sweden:** clearest recovery, at about +4% a year for 2026-27, with housing starts rising from about 26,500 (2024) towards 33,500-36,000.
- **Norway:** housing starts are at their lowest since 1946 (about 15,500-15,700 in 2025). A modest recovery is expected in 2026, with RMI stable.

### Cited Findings
**Euroconstruct (Nov 2025, 100th conference)**
- Euroconstruct-area output +0.3% in 2025 and about **+2.4% in 2026**. Civil engineering is the main engine, and residential stays weak through 2026 before recovering in 2027. **Sweden** is one of four countries expected to exceed **4% annual growth in 2026-2028**. [PRESS release; detailed tables paywalled] — [Euroconstruct press release](https://www.euroconstruct.org/?p=10016217); [Euroconstruct summary report page](https://www.euroconstruct.org/ec_reports/summary-report/)
- Denmark in a Euroconstruct-based table: **-12.0% (2023), -4.3% (2024), +1.2% (2025), +2.0% (2026)**. [SNIPPET; edition unverified] — [WIFO / Euroconstruct](https://www.wifo.ac.at/publication/438675/)

**Denmark**
- Dansk Erhverv (14 Sep 2026): construction sector **+3.3% in 2026** (revised up 1.0 pp). **Public construction investment +27.3%**, **residential investment -2.5%**, business construction -0.5%. Commercial construction starts in H1 2026 were +25% in m². Residential and business investment "look positive for 2027". [HARD] — [Dansk Erhverv press release](https://www.danskerhverv.dk/presse-og-nyheder/nyheder/2026/september/pressemeddelelse-byggeprognose/)
- Renovation market expected **+7% in 2025 and +6% in 2026**. [SNIPPET; DI/Dansk Erhverv forecast] — [DI Byggeri prognoser](https://www.danskindustri.dk/brancher/di-byggeri/analyse-og-politik/analyser-af-bygge--og-anlagsbranchen/prognoser-for-bygge--og-anlagsbranchen/)
- Danmarks Statistik housing starts (seasonally adjusted): **Q4 2025 5,450** (-18% q/q), **Q1 2026 5,173** (-19%), **Q2 2026 6,714 (+23%)**. Total started floor area in Q2 2026 was 1.7 m m², led by residential (+14%). [HARD, 14 Aug 2026] — [DST Nyt 52827](https://www.dst.dk/nyt/52827); [DST Nyt 51858](https://www.dst.dk/nyt/51858)
- Danish construction estimated to have shrunk 1.1% in real terms in 2025 (GlobalData/ResearchAndMarkets). This is a commercial estimate and conflicts with the Euroconstruct table above. [PRESS] — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260127162984/en/Denmark-Construction-Industry-Report-2025-Output-Shrinks-by-1.1-Owing-to-High-Inflation-Elevated-Costs-of-Materials-and-Supply-Chain-Disruptions---Forecasts-to-2029---ResearchAndMarkets.com)
- STARK: "In the Nordics, one market rebounded in less than a year, while others are recovering after downturns lasting 18-24 months." [HARD] — [STARK AR 2025](https://www.stark-deutschland.de/fileadmin/Homepage/nachhaltigkeit/dokumente/nachhaltigkeitsberichte/STARK_Group_Annual_Report_2025.pdf)

**Sweden**
- Byggföretagen (Oct 2025): building investment **-2% in 2025, +4% in 2026**. Housing +1% / +5%, commercial premises -4% / +1%, infrastructure 0% / +8%. New-build housing investment +11% in 2026. **ROT (renovation) rising moderately in 2025, dampened in 2026 when the ROT deduction is reduced.** Housing starts (apartments): **26,500 (2024) → 28,400 (2025) → 30,500 (2026)**. [PRESS] — [Placera / Byggföretagen](https://www.placera.se/telegram/byggforetagen-bygginvesteringar-2-2025-4-2026-20251022)
- Byggföretagen (31 Mar 2026): investment **+4% a year in 2026 and 2027** (about +8% cumulative), to about **SEK 704 bn in 2027**. Employment +2.7% (about 10,000 jobs) between 2025 and 2027. Housing starts **33,500 (2026) and 35,900 (2027)**. The CEO sees "a recovery in all sectors". ## 5. E-commerce / online share; click & collect; digital B2B ordering

### Takeaway
Online B2C penetration in building materials is low but material. Byggmax, the most transparent reporter, gets **18% of sales online** (22% outside Sweden). Byggmax itself says e-commerce share is lower than in other retail because the goods are bulky. B2B digital ordering (EDI and webshop) is more advanced: 33% of Danish company revenue comes through online channels, mostly EDI. Builders merchants mainly compete on next-day delivery and logistics rather than on publishing online-share KPIs.

### Cited Findings
- **Byggmax online sales share 2025: 18% of group (2024: 18%). Sweden 18%, other Nordics 22% (2024: 23%).** "Online" includes orders on any Byggmax e-commerce site, which covers click-and-collect and delivery, plus all Skånska Byggvaror sales. In 2025 Byggmax streamlined the broader e-commerce assortment, which hurt sales but lifted margins. [HARD] — [Byggmax Year-end report 2025, Note 1](https://mb.cision.com/Main/109/4299915/3906835.pdf)
- Byggmax on the industry: "The share of e-commerce is lower than in other retail, as building materials are bulky and customers often make many purchases during a project. Proximity to physical stores is therefore crucial." Delivery is either to the home or by in-store collection. [HARD] — [Byggmax AR 2025](https://mb.cision.com/Main/109/4328714/4017358.pdf)
- Denmark (DI, 2025): **33% of companies' total revenue comes from online sales channels**, of which **25 pp is EDI and 8 pp is web sales** (webshop, app, marketplaces). 30% of companies have web sales. This covers all industries, not building materials specifically. [SNIPPET] — [DI E-commerce analysis 2025](https://www.danskindustri.dk/vi-radgiver-dig/ecommerce/nyhedsarkiv/nyheder/2025/10/dansk-e-handel-2025-onlinekanaler-satter-ny-omsatningsrekord/)
- STARK Group: digital transformation programme **"Athene"** (started 2022) modernises ERP, replenishment, warehouse management, sales and order handling. It runs an AI "Anton Branch Assistant" for branch staff, digital climate tools (DK, SE, and rolled out to NO in 2024/25), and **next-day delivery across the full offering** from the Swedish light-hub. **No digital share of sales is disclosed.** [HARD] — [STARK AR 2025](https://www.stark-deutschland.de/fileadmin/Homepage/nachhaltigkeit/dokumente/nachhaltigkeitsberichte/STARK_Group_Annual_Report_2025.pdf)
- Sweden B2C: PostNord's E-barometern puts the building-products category at about 2% of consumers shopping online in a given month (Jan 2025, vs 3% a year earlier). [SNIPPET; secondary figures unclear] — [PostNord E-barometern 2025](https://www.postnord.com/insights/reports/e-barometer-2025-annual-report/)
- Norway: online sales of building materials were cited as about 3% of the market "a few years ago". [SNIPPET, OLD, unverified] — no reliable primary source found.
- Ahlsell (B2B installation distributor): online channels are reportedly about 50% of turnover. [SNIPPET, unverified; Ahlsell 2017 release only says "significant share"] — [Ahlsell](https://www.ahlsellgroup.com/en/news/ahlsell-strengthens-position-within-online-sales-2751067)

### Inferences
- For Company A, a credible benchmark is **about 10-20% of B2C sales online, including click & collect**. B2B digital ordering is likely higher where EDI or webshops are used by larger contractors, but SME tradespeople still order heavily in-branch and by phone. This is an inference; no Nordic merchant publishes a B2B digital share.
- The leaders' differentiator is logistics: next-day delivery, central light-hubs and 24/7 branches, more than webshop share.

### Gaps
- No published digital-sales % for STARK, Beijer, Bygma, Optimera, Byggmakker or XL-BYGG.
- No survey of digital ordering adoption among Nordic contractors was found.

## 6. Competitive intensity: price pressure, consolidation, margins, delivery as differentiator

### Takeaway
Margins are thin and under pressure:
- STARK Nordics adj. EBITDA is **6.6%**, down from 8.9% in FY21/22.
- Bygma profit has fallen for four straight years.
- Johannes Fog is loss-making.
- Bauhaus is leaving Norway.

Consolidation is accelerating (STARK, Kesko/Davidsen, XL-BYG/Fog, Kesko/Dahl). Leaders differentiate on delivery and logistics (next-day delivery, central hubs) and on sustainability documentation.

### Cited Findings
- **STARK Nordics EBITDA margin: 8.9% (FY21/22) → 8.1% → 6.5% → 6.6% (FY24/25).** Gross margin is steady at 26.4-26.9%. Wage inflation and branch expansion raised staff costs by 7.3%. [HARD] — [STARK AR 2025](https://www.stark-deutschland.de/fileadmin/Homepage/nachhaltigkeit/dokumente/nachhaltigkeitsberichte/STARK_Group_Annual_Report_2025.pdf)
- STARK calls the downturn the "most severe and prolonged in over 30 years". Its above-branch light-hub in Sweden aims to "improve gross margins" with "next-day delivery across our full offering". Its strategy includes "best-in-class delivery services" and on-time-in-full (OTIF) delivery. [HARD] — same source
- **Bygma:** record revenue, but profit fell for a fourth year. Margins are pressured by weak demand and higher operating costs, and sales growth came from acquisitions and pricing. [PRESS] — [Interior Daily](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9832326/bygma-revenue-reaches-new-high-as-profits-remain-under-pressure/)
- **Johannes Fog:** loss of DKK 14.9 m in 2025. It joined XL-BYG to strengthen purchasing scale. [HARD] — [Fog FY2025 release](https://www.johannesfog.dk/media/vpxnh1yh/pressemeddelelse-aarsregnskab-2025.pdf)
- **Byggmax:** EBITA margin recovered to 5.9% (target ≥7%), helped by product margins, e-commerce assortment optimisation and early-payment discounts. Its annual report lists price pressure from competitors as a risk. [HARD] — [Byggmax AR 2025](https://mb.cision.com/Main/109/4328714/4017358.pdf)
- **Bauhaus:** exiting Norway after NOK 870 m in cumulative losses. Large-box formats are pressured by high rates, property, staffing and inventory costs. [PRESS] — [Dagens PS](https://www.dagensps.se/foretag/bauhaus-lagger-ner-alla-varuhus-i-norge-vad-hander-i-sverige/)
- **Consolidation examples:**
  - Davidsen/Kesko tripled-up acquisitions in DK, giving about a 20% share. — [Kesko release](https://inderes.se/releases/k-ryhma-kesko-to-significantly-expand-operations-in-denmark-by-acquiring-three-central-building-and-home-improvement-trade-operators)
  - STARK: 13 acquisitions in the Nordics since 2021. — [STARK AR 2025](https://www.stark-deutschland.de/fileadmin/Homepage/nachhaltigkeit/dokumente/nachhaltigkeitsberichte/STARK_Group_Annual_Report_2025.pdf)
  - Kesko is buying Dahl SE/NO/DK. — [Inderes](https://www.inderes.dk/en/releases/inside-information-kesko-to-strengthen-its-technical-trade-by-acquiring-dahls-operations-in-sweden-norway-and-denmark)
  - In Norway, Optimera and Byggmakker have made repeated bolt-ons. — [Konkurransetilsynet filings](https://konkurransetilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/2023_0238-1-OFF-Optimera-AS-Bygg-Gros-AS.-m-fl.pdf)
- Norwegian competition authority: the builders-merchant market is "characterised by strong competition between several large chains". [HARD, OLD] — [Konkurransetilsynet 2021](https://konkurransetilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/2021_0118-1-OFF-Lovenskiold-Handel-AS-Ivar-O.-Haugen-AS.pdf)

### Inferences
- With EBITDA margins of about 5-7% and gross margins flat, cost-to-serve (delivery, branch labour) is the main profit lever. That makes delivery efficiency, route optimisation and digital ordering credible themes for a consulting proposal.
- Scale players with central hubs (STARK) are using delivery speed and range as weapons. A Company A without hubs risks share loss among pro customers.

### Gaps
- No market-wide price index or margin series for Nordic builders merchants was found.
- Delivery-share metrics (% of B2B orders delivered to site) are not disclosed by any player found.
